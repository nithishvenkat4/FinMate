## 1. Principles of Prudent Investing

Investing in financial markets involves trade-offs between expected returns, liquidity, and capital loss risk. SEBI mandates that market participants understand the intrinsic characteristics of diverse financial asset classes.

### 1.1 Risk and Return Continuum
- **Risk-Return Tradeoff**: High returns are inextricably linked with higher potential volatility and downside risk. Guarantees of high, fixed, or risk-free returns in market-linked products are deceptive and indicative of fraudulent schemes.
- **Capital Preservation**: Fixed-income instruments (Government Securities, Public Provident Fund, High-Grade Corporate Bonds, Bank FDs) prioritize capital preservation over capital appreciation.
- **Wealth Creation**: Equities and equity mutual funds offer long-term inflation-beating wealth creation potential, but experience short-to-medium term drawdown volatility.

### 1.2 The Principle of Diversification
Diversification is the primary safeguard against idiosyncratic risk:
- Investors must allocate capital across uncorrelated asset classes, including domestic equities, debt instruments, precious metals (Gold and Silver ETFs/Sovereign Gold Bonds), and cash equivalents.
- Concentration risk arises when an excessive percentage of wealth is allocated to a single asset, single employer stock, or concentrated sector.
- Systematic Investment Plans (SIPs) in mutual funds mitigate market-timing risk via rupee-cost averaging across economic cycles.

## 2. Investor Rights and Due Diligence

### 2.1 Risk Profiling and Suitability
- Financial products recommended or chosen should strictly align with the investor's age, investment horizon, cash flow predictability, and emotional risk tolerance.
- Money required within a short timeframe (less than 1 to 3 years), such as tuition fees, down-payment on housing, or emergency reserves, should not be deployed in volatile equity markets.
- Unhedged derivatives (Futures & Options) carry substantial systemic leverage where a high percentage of individual retail traders incur net losses. Derivatives must not be used as gambling or speculative short-term instruments.

### 2.2 Transparency and Redressal
- All regulated entities (Stock Brokers, Depository Participants, Investment Advisers, Research Analysts) must provide clear disclosure of fees, commissions, execution terms, and potential conflicts of interest.
- Grievances can be lodged through the SEBI SCORES portal and the SMART ODR platform for online dispute resolution.

## 1. The Impact of Inflation on Purchasing Power

Inflation represents the annualized percentage rate at which the general level of prices for goods and services rises, progressively eroding the real purchasing power of uninvested currency.

### 1.1 Nominal vs. Real Rate of Return
A common cognitive error in personal finance is evaluating investment returns purely on nominal metrics without factoring in consumer inflation and taxation:
- **Formula**:
  $$\text{Real Return} \approx \text{Nominal Return} - \text{Inflation Rate} - \text{Taxes}$$
- **The Cash Drag**: Holding excessive cash in a basic savings account earning 3% per annum during a period of 6% Consumer Price Index (CPI) inflation results in an annual loss of approximately 3% in real purchasing power. Over a 10-year span, ₹1,00,000 in idle cash loses over 25% of its real goods-and-services purchasing value.

## 2. Compound Interest and The Time Value of Money

Compound interest is the exponential mathematical process where interest earned on principal is reinvested, generating interest upon accumulated interest in subsequent compounding periods.

### 2.1 The Rule of 72
A quick mental approximation to determine the number of years required for an invested sum to double at a given annual compound return ($r\%$):
$$\text{Years to Double} \approx \frac{72}{r}$$
- At 6% annual return (conservative debt/FD): doubles in $\approx 12$ years.
- At 12% annual return (diversified equity index): doubles in $\approx 6$ years.

### 2.2 The Asymmetry of Early Savings (Compounding Horizon)
The most critical variable in wealth accumulation is the **time horizon** ($t$), because exponential growth accelerates during later compounding decades:
- An investor who starts investing ₹10,000 monthly at age 22 accumulates vastly higher wealth by age 50 than an investor who starts investing ₹25,000 monthly at age 35, despite the late starter contributing substantially more total capital.
- Consistent, disciplined contributions via automated SIPs harness compound growth while minimizing emotional timing errors.
